The two kinds of homes we work in

Older Belleville: downtown, the West End and Signal Hill

In 62220 and 62223, many homes are brick houses and bungalows that have stood for a century or more. They sit on limestone or brick foundations with no drain tile, have partial cellars or crawl spaces, plaster-and-lath walls, galvanized supply pipes and cast-iron drains. Floor drains connect to aging clay-tile sewer laterals. In a hard storm the sewer system overloads and the lowest drain in the house becomes the exit point. That is why sewer backup is the most common emergency call in this part of town.

Newer Belleville: east and north subdivisions

In 62221 and 62226, toward Frank Scott Parkway, Green Mount Road and Swansea, homes are newer, with poured foundations, finished basements and sump pits. A crew that treats both the same way gets one of them wrong. Plaster is dried differently from drywall, and a brick foundation cannot be handled like poured concrete.

What we do when we arrive

  1. Make it safe

    We check for electrical hazards, identify whether the water is clean, gray or sewage, and keep people and pets out of contaminated areas.

  2. Stop and remove the water

    Pumps handle deep water in cellars and basements. Extractors pull water from carpet, pad and hard floors. On old masonry foundations, deep water is lowered in stages so the walls are not stressed.

  3. Find where it went

    Moisture meters and thermal imaging show water inside plaster, behind paneling, under hardwood and in joist bays, so nothing wet is left hidden.

  4. Clean and dry

    Contaminated materials are removed and surfaces disinfected. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until daily readings show the structure is dry.

  5. Put it back

    Plaster and drywall repair, insulation, trim and flooring are completed after drying so you are not left with open walls.

Why Belleville basements get wet

Three things come up again and again. First, older foundations were built to be damp. Stone and brick walls let groundwater seep through, and without drain tile there is nothing to carry it away. Second, the sewers in the older neighborhoods are easily overloaded in heavy rain, which pushes water back up private laterals. Third, parts of St. Clair County sit over old coal mine workings, and ground movement can crack foundations and break buried water and sewer lines.

Water damage repair in Belleville, IL has to account for all three. Drying a room is only half the job if the cause is a cracked lateral or a foundation that seeps every time it rains, so we tell you what we find and what a plumber or foundation contractor would need to address.

What to do while the crew is on the way

  • If a pipe broke, close the main valve, usually where the water line enters the basement.
  • If the floor drain is backing up, stop using sinks, toilets, the washer and the dishwasher. Every gallon you send down comes back up.
  • Keep out of sewage and floodwater. Do not let children or pets near it.
  • Switch off power to the wet area only if the panel can be reached from a dry floor.
  • Lift what you can off the floor: boxes, rugs, electronics.
  • Photograph each room and the water line on the walls.
  • Leave the furnace and water heater off if water reached them.

Insurance, sewer backup and mine subsidence

Standard homeowners policies generally cover sudden pipe and appliance failures. Sewer and drain backup needs its own endorsement, and outside flooding needs a flood policy. In Illinois, mine subsidence coverage is a separate item on the policy and applies to ground movement, not to water itself. We do not determine coverage. We document what happened so your insurer can.

The crew is dispatched immediately after your call, any time of day or night. You are given an expected arrival time on the phone.

Can a wet plaster wall be saved?

Often it can. Plaster tolerates water better than drywall if it is dried slowly and evenly. Plaster that has turned soft, crumbly or has pulled away from the lath is removed.

Is water in my basement after rain normal for an old Belleville house?

Seepage is common in stone and brick foundations, but standing water, sewage odor or water from the floor drain is not something to wait on.

How long does it take to dry an older home?

Plan on four to seven days. Dense materials such as plaster, brick and thick lumber dry more slowly than modern drywall and framing.

Do wet plaster-and-lath walls in a Belleville home dry out, or do they have to be torn out?

They often dry out. Plaster that stays hard and bonded to the lath can be dried in place. It is removed only where it has softened, crumbled, separated or been soaked by sewage.

This matters because replacing plaster with drywall changes the look and feel of an old house, and is more work than drying. Saving sound plaster is usually the better result and the lower cost.

How we decide what stays

Moisture meter readings across the wall, compared with a dry wall in the same house.

A tap and press test for hollow or loose areas.

The type of water. Clean water allows drying. Sewage and floodwater do not.

How long it was wet. Days of saturation rot wood lath.

What is inside the wall. Old walls often have no insulation, which helps drying. Blown-in insulation that got wet has to come out.

Drywall

When it can be dried

Clean water, short exposure, no swelling or sagging, and no insulation soaked behind it.

When it is cut out

When it has swollen, crumbled, grown mold, or wicked dirty water. A flood cut removes the lower section in a straight line, usually 12 to 24 inches above the highest wet reading, so the cavity can dry and the new piece joins cleanly.

Insulation

Wet fiberglass batts are removed. They hold water against the framing and do not dry in a closed wall.

Plaster

Drying in place

Baseboards come off, and small holes drilled behind them let dry air into the cavity. Dehumidifiers work slowly so the plaster does not crack.

Patching

Damaged areas are cut back to solid plaster, the lath is checked and replaced if rotted, and the hole is filled with plaster or with drywall set flush, then finished with a skim coat blended into the surrounding wall.

Finish

Old walls are rarely flat. The patch is feathered wide so it follows the wall's existing surface.

Baseboards, trim and doors

Older homes have tall wood baseboards and wide casings that are worth saving. They are removed carefully, numbered, dried flat and reinstalled. Modern MDF trim swells when wet and is replaced. Water damaged baseboards that are solid wood usually survive.

Paneling and wallpaper

Basement paneling and old wallpaper trap moisture and often hide mold. Paneling is removed to dry the wall behind. Wallpaper over wet plaster is taken off. See Mold Remediation if growth is found.

Rebuilding

Water damage reconstruction in Belleville, IL follows drying, never before it. New insulation where it existed. Drywall or plaster repair. Tape, mud and sanding with dust control. Primer and paint, corner to corner, so the color matches. Trim reinstalled and caulked. Ceilings are covered on our Ceiling Water Damage Repair page, and floors on Hardwood Floor Water Damage.

Old paint and safety

Pre-1978 homes may have lead-based paint. When painted plaster or trim must be cut or sanded, lead-safe practices are used.

Cost and insurance

A small section of wall typically costs a few hundred to a couple of thousand dollars to dry and repair. Several rooms, plaster restoration and trim work raise the figure. When the water loss is covered, the repair of walls is normally part of the same claim.

Wall repair questions

Is wet drywall removal always necessary?

No. Lightly wetted drywall from clean water can be dried. Swollen or moldy drywall is removed.

What is a flood cut drywall repair?

A straight horizontal cut above the wet line. The lower section is replaced and the seam taped, which is quicker and neater than patching irregular holes.

How do I know the wall is dry inside?

Readings are taken through small holes or with non-invasive meters, and recorded daily.

Will the patch show?

A properly feathered and painted repair should not be noticeable.

Can you do plaster water damage repair on walls with curves or arches?

Yes. Curved work takes longer but can be matched.

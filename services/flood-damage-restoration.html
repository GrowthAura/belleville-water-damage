Which parts of Belleville flood when Richland Creek rises?

Low-lying streets and properties along the Richland Creek corridor through the center and south of the city are most exposed. Elsewhere, flooding comes from overwhelmed storm drains and runoff, not the creek.

You can check a specific address on the FEMA flood map or with St. Clair County. Many properties that flood are outside mapped flood zones, because short, intense rain overwhelms street drainage anywhere there is a low spot.

How flood water gets in

Overland flow across yards into cellar doors, window wells and coal chutes.

Storm drains that back up into the street and over the curb.

Creek water rising into yards and garages.

Sewers surcharging at the same time, pushing sewage up floor drains. See Sewer Backup Cleanup.

Groundwater rising through the floors of old cellars.

In a major storm, a house may be dealing with two or three of these at once.

What makes floodwater a special case

It is treated as Category 3, contaminated water. It picks up sewage, oil, fertilizer and debris. Soft materials that it soaks are removed. Everything that stays is washed, disinfected and dried.

Step by step

Safety check

Power is confirmed off. Gas appliances are left off. Structural concerns, such as a shifted foundation wall, are noted before work.

Water out

Pumps and extractors remove standing water. On old masonry cellars the level is lowered gradually.

Mud out

Silt and debris are shovelled and washed out before they dry hard.

Tear out

Carpet, pad, paneling, drywall and insulation that floodwater reached are removed. Plaster is evaluated wall by wall.

Clean and disinfect

Floors, masonry and framing are scrubbed and disinfected.

Dry

Dehumidifiers and air movers run for a week or more, because brick, stone and old lumber hold so much water.

Rebuild

Walls and floors are restored once readings are dry.

Flooded cellars in older homes

Most older Belleville cellars are unfinished, which limits what has to be torn out. The work is mainly pumping, mud-out, disposing of ruined stored items, disinfecting, and drying the masonry and the floor framing above. The furnace, water heater and electrical panel need checking by the right trades. See Basement Flood Cleanup.

Businesses

Ground-floor shops and restaurants face the added pressure of lost trading days. We work extended hours and in phases so part of the space can reopen sooner. See Commercial Water Damage Restoration.

Storm damage from above

High winds and hail open roofs, and rain follows. Storm damage restoration in Belleville, IL includes drying attics, taking down wet ceilings and protecting the interior until the roof is repaired.

Insurance

Rising water from outside is excluded by homeowners policies and covered by flood insurance, if you have it. Sewer backup during the same storm falls under a backup endorsement. Roof damage from wind is a standard homeowners claim. We record each cause separately, with photos and measurements, so claims go to the right policy.

Time and cost

Removal and cleaning take one to several days. Drying takes a week or longer in masonry buildings. Costs depend on depth and finish level. An unfinished cellar is typically in the low thousands of dollars. Finished living space or commercial space is more. A written scope comes first.

Flood questions

When is it safe to go back in?

When the water has receded and power is confirmed off. Wear boots and gloves.

Does flash flood cleanup need disinfection even if the water looked clear?

Yes. Clear-looking floodwater is still contaminated.

Do you handle flood water removal from cellars with outside doors?

Yes. Exterior cellar doors make pumping and hauling easier.

What about creek flooding cleanup in garages and sheds?

We clean and disinfect those as well.

Can plaster walls survive a flood?

Sometimes, if the water was shallow and brief. Plaster soaked by contaminated water for a long period is usually removed.
